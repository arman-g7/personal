I'm 17, in high school in Isfahan, Iran. I want to write this down honestly,
partly for whoever reads it, and partly for myself — so I have something to
look back on later and check whether I actually did what I said I would.

I got into programming young, around the start of 7th grade, and spent a
couple of years trying out very different areas before landing on blockchain.
For about 2.5 years now I've been deep in Solidity, smart contract security,
and competitive auditing — Code4rena, HackenProof, that world. I've built
several smart contract projects, done freelance full-stack web work to
support myself, and even co-authored a conference paper on post-quantum
cryptography migration for Ethereum accounts. None of that was wasted time.
It taught me how to actually build things, how to think about security, and
how to work independently without someone handing me a syllabus.

But somewhere in the last year, I realized this isn't the thing I want to
spend my whole career on. What I actually care about is further out: the
intersection of AI, healthcare, and neuroscience. Not AI as a buzzword — I
mean the actual hard problem of modeling biological systems well enough that
we can run real experiments on digital versions of them instead of only on
carbon-based ones. That field is still young, and most of the people in it
come from biology or medicine rather than from an algorithmic/engineering
background, which is exactly the gap I think I can fill.

So I'm rebuilding my foundations: Python, real math (calculus, linear
algebra, probability), classical machine learning, deep learning, and
eventually LLM/agent systems — in that order, on purpose, instead of jumping
around. I'm applying it already: I'm working on the machine learning side of
a friend's pharmacy PhD thesis, using Bayesian optimization and random
forests to help predict good formulations from experimental data. It's a
small project, but it's real, and it's the actual shape of the work I want
to keep doing.

The plan for the next few years is straightforward, even if it's not the
conventional path here: finish a degree at Azad University without pretending
it's the main event, put the real effort into self-study, open-source
projects, and research output, and use that to apply for a fully-funded
master's program in Europe — hopefully somewhere that lets me keep pushing
toward AI applied to health and neuroscience.

I don't have this all figured out. I've changed direction before and I might
again. But I'd rather write down clearly what I'm actually doing and why,
than dress it up as something more finished than it is.
